You were nature
before you
had a name.

The stone keeps still. The grass bends, and returns. Two koi swim on: one toward its work, one toward what it loves. So do you.

We compose less
than we remove.

Follow the curve of a stem. Remove what draws the eye away. The space left behind lets each remaining form become clearer.

Try letting go

The third stem
is empty space.

Release the extra stem. Watch the water respond, then notice the room between the bud and leaf.

Four studies
in change.

Bloom, reach, fall, rest. Four imagined seasons, each finding a different balance.

Before the stem,
the vessel.

An opening changes the way a stem stands.
A rim gives the composition its horizon.

A low horizon.

A wide opening reveals where stems meet and lets their lines spread.

A line, lifted.

A narrow opening gathers the stems and directs the eye upward.

Weight, then air.

A rounded body anchors the composition; a small stem changes its silhouette.

The room
is part of the work.

In this imagined room, shadows extend the arrangement across the wall. The space around the bowl gives its small gesture presence.

What time leaves.
What space reveals.

Petal, clay, stone and weathered wood—each made clearer by the space around it.

One study is unavailable. The remaining studies stay visible.

There is more
in less.

Look at the white blossom. Then return to the branch.

Enough, and room around it.

Ma is an independent digital concept by XI, exploring how an interface can make space felt. The botanical scenes are imagined studies, including generated imagery selected and edited for this work. They do not document a working florist, a client commission, or a physical installation.

The water is a response to your attention. Once it has answered, it becomes still. That is the idea behind the work: an experience can feel complete without filling every moment.
